# Chapter 6 — Limitations and Production Relevance (draft v1, 2026-11-12)

## 6.1 Internal validity

1. **Package contrast, not a pure gate effect.** The DMN village differs from the CoT village in three things: its architecture (DMN → TPN → gate), its vote prompt (the CoT prompt alone ends with "endless delay also loses games") and its token budget. The primary estimates the effect of the package. The ablation cells are what attribute any part of it to the gate (§10, budget permitting).
2. **The tested gate is a confidence threshold.** The EVT term cannot fire under the frozen constants (D-001). Any claim about "expectation-violation gating" is unsupported by this study.
3. **Verification shrinks confidence almost uniformly.** In non-confirmatory traces, TPN lowered confidence by about 0.21–0.25 for guilty and innocent suspects alike, and slightly *reduced* discrimination (paired AUC −0.05; D-005). Delays may therefore reflect a global shift more than selective doubt. The post-lock within-DMN replication tests this.
4. **The compute match is approximate.** CoT-x3 matches vote sampling, not total tokens. The measured per-game costs per arm are reported (calibration report and `nuisance.md`).
5. **Several outcomes are mechanical.** Delay quality and S2 judge a delay by the realised plurality target. A blinded judge sample (§13) is required to separate "evidence did not justify acting" from "lucky avoidance". It needs a human annotator, or an LLM judge from a different model family.
6. **Hidden-information leak in the old code.** Pilot and smoke-team-01 DMN trust carried the hidden count of living mafia (D-004, fixed in P0-E2). Pilot calibration numbers (ECE 0.105) are not comparable and may be optimistic.

## 6.2 External validity

1. **One backbone, one opponent.** All claims are restricted to `meta/muse-spark-1.3-contributor` and to a CoT mafia. Nothing is claimed about DMN mafia, other models, ReAct or Constitution agents.
2. **Mafia models the decision, not the blast radius.** In about 8 of 16 real incidents, the decisive error was acting on an unverifiable belief, and Mafia captures that. Most vendor remediations were platform controls (soft delete, scoped tokens, environment separation, peer review), which Mafia does not model at all (`docs/research/incidents.md`).
3. **The bridge is small and simulated.** IrrevOps has 60 tasks in 6 families, 2 repetitions, simulated tools and one backbone, and it reports estimates only. Its external gate (B2) reads authoritative platform metadata by construction. Its strength therefore shows what a platform *can* enforce when such metadata exists, not what a model can infer.
4. **Task cues are a design choice.** After a pooled ceiling in the bridge smoke run, the hidden-state cues were made subtler (D7). Absolute harm rates depend on cue strength. Contrasts between arms on the same tasks are the robust quantity.

## 6.3 Statistical limits

- At n = 184 trios, power for a +10-point win-rate effect is about 0.49 (**derived**, §5). A null primary is likely even if a modest effect exists, so the CI is the result.
- The secondary families are Holm-adjusted within the family. Everything else is descriptive.
- Game-clustered CIs treat seats within a game as dependent. Seed pairing removes role-assignment variance but not the sampling noise of the LLM.

## 6.4 Production relevance: what a practitioner can take away

**Adoptable artifact.** The `src/safety` package contains:

- `PolicyGate` (deterministic policy-as-code);
- `SafetyGate` (threshold gate);
- `guard_tool` (wraps any tool call);
- `AuditLogger` (one row per decision, logged to `logs/safety_audit.parquet`).

**Evidence-ordered recommendations.** **[after unblinding]** Each is to be confirmed or withdrawn by the results.

1. **Enforce outside the model first.**
   - Block irreversible calls on targets that are unverified in this session or marked protected by the platform (B2).
   - This is supported by prior work (GateArena: 91/400 → 0/400 violations) and by our bridge estimate **[after unblinding]**.
   - The external gate's cost is false holds when the agent does not verify. Measure it.
2. **Prompt rules are not controls.** Measure B1 against B0 on your own tasks before relying on a written rule. The incident record contains three violations of explicit rules (INC-01, 02, 12).
3. **A model-side verify-then-delay gate is a complement, not a substitute.** Use it where no authoritative metadata exists. Report its harm reduction *together with* its false-hold rate. Our Mafia S1/S2 pair and the bridge B3 contrasts give the size of each **[after unblinding]**.
4. **Log the attempt, not just the outcome.** Runtime-contract studies show that blocked calls are still attempted. The audit log records every attempted irreversible call, its verification state and the gate decision.

**Recalibration checklist (replaces the v0 checklist in `docs/enterprise_integration.md`).**

1. Build 20–30 *paired* tasks from your own incident history. Each pair needs a should-act twin and a should-abstain twin with the same surface request.
2. Run the bare agent, the agent with a prompt rule, the external gate and the verify-delay gate on the same pairs, with at least 2 repetitions.
3. Report the harmful-act rate and the false-hold rate side by side, with paired CIs over task pairs. Never report harm alone.
4. Fix thresholds *before* the evaluation run, and do not tune them on the evaluation pairs. If you must tune, hold out pairs.
5. Make the protected-resource metadata that the external gate relies on authoritative: production tags, sole-backup flags, freeze windows and payee approval. A wrong or stale tag defeats the gate silently.
6. Give every DELAY or ESCALATE an owner and an SLA. A gate without a human path turns into denial of service.
7. Re-run the paired evaluation after any model, prompt or tool change. Treat a change in false-hold rate as a regression, just like a change in harm rate.
