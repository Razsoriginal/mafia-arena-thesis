Draft, searched 2026-11-12. Scope letters: (b) tool-use / agent-safety benchmarks, (c) runtime guardrails and external enforcement, (e) social-deduction / deception benchmarks, (g) standards and frameworks.

Verification levels:
- **read-full**: a primary page or document read in full, or a substantial part of the full text.
- **abstract**: the arXiv abs page or venue page was fetched and read.
- **abstract (snippet)**: only abstract text that search results quoted from arXiv or the venue; the page itself was not fetched.
- **unverified**: secondary sources only (vendor blogs, press, aggregators).

Numbers are taken from the version stated. Several papers change their numbers between arXiv versions, so cite the version. Anything published in 2026 is an arXiv preprint unless a venue is given. None of the numbers below were re-derived from tables.

## 2. Is there an existing benchmark of paired should-act/should-abstain irreversible-tool tasks under adversarial pressure?

**Partly. No single benchmark found covers all three properties. Two benchmarks together cover most of it.**

- **AgentAbstain (arXiv:2607.10059, Jul 2026)** already provides 263 *paired* should-act/should-abstain tasks with simulated irreversible "commit-class" tools (delete_file, send_bank_transfer, send_email, flight cancel) across 42 MCP sandboxes. It has a "High-Stakes Action" scenario and a post-hoc-abstention failure mode (act, then claim refusal). Its own scope section **excludes adversarial prompt injection**. It runs no external-gate baseline and no calibration or delay mechanism, and it runs each task once.
- **SentryBench (arXiv:2610.34535, Oct 2026)** is adversarial (real injection/jailbreak payloads) with a benign control arm and cost-sensitive over-refusal / over-reaction metrics. It compares a trust-gating wrapper against "refuse-more" prompting. Its domain is public safety (dispatch/alerts), not DevOps/DB/payment, and it does not frame tasks as paired irreversible ops.
- **GateArena (NeurIPS 2026 workshop)** covers irreversible tools with request-intent deviation (an adversarial-like mismatch) and compares an environment-enforced confirmation against prompt-level instruction. It uses only two action types and is not a paired benchmark.
- Adjacent: AgentHarm (harmful/benign pairs, but malicious users); OpenAgentSafety (benign/malicious/hidden intent with real tools); Refusal-Transfer (matched harmful/benign, not executed); UnderSpecBench / DBA-Bench / SABER / SNARE (DevOps/DB destructive ops without paired abstention under attack); InjecAgent/AgentDojo (adversarial, but they score attack success rather than calibrated abstention).

**Implication for the planned sandbox.** A generic "paired should-act/should-abstain irreversible tools" sandbox would largely duplicate AgentAbstain. To be novel, it would need to add the elements AgentAbstain explicitly lacks:
1. Adversarial or deceptive pressure (injection, social engineering, conflicting forged evidence).
2. An external gate vs prompt-rule vs no-gate ablation.
3. A calibrated DELAY option with a cost model (as in SentryBench).
4. Compute-matched baselines (CoT-x3) and random/majority null floors.
5. Repeated seeds.

Reusing or extending AgentAbstain environments (license permitting) or AgentDojo suites may be cheaper than building a new sandbox. That would also make the work a replication-plus-extension rather than a competing benchmark.

## 3. External enforcement vs in-model restraint: what evidence exists?

Evidence favouring external enforcement, roughly strongest first (all preprints or self-reported unless noted):
- **GateArena (NeurIPS-W 2026).** Under a prompt-level confirmation instruction, agents still called the withheld tool in 38% / 48% / <1% of episodes, depending on the backbone. An in-handler check cut protocol-violating executions from 91/400 to 0/400. Irreversible harm fell from 37.5–60% (ungated) to 2.5% (gated).
- **Progent.** Deterministic privilege policies cut AgentDojo ASR from 39.9% to 1.0%, with utility 79.4% → 76.3%.
- **CaMeL.** Design-level enforcement solves 77% of tasks with provable security, vs 84% undefended.
- **LlamaFirewall.** Layered external scanners cut AgentDojo ASR from 17.6% to 1.75%.
- **Symbolic guardrails (CMU).** 74% of τ²/CAR/MedAgent requirements are symbolically enforceable, and enforcing them did not reduce utility.
- **SentryBench.** "Refuse-more" caution (a prompt/in-model approach) pushed over-refusal to 100% on two of three models for at most a 3.7 pt ASR gain, and gave the worst harm. A trust-gating wrapper gave the lowest harm on every model.

Evidence that in-model or prompt restraint is unreliable:
- **Mind the GAP.** 219 text-refuse/tool-execute cases persist under safety-reinforced system prompts.
- **LITMUS.** "Execution Hallucination": the agent refuses verbally after the operation has already run.
- **Refusal Transfer.** 10–52% of chat-refused requests are executed as tool calls. Activation steering trades off unsafe calls against benign use.
- **Tool affordance.** Perfect text compliance, but up to 85% violations once tools are available.
- **SafeAgentBench.** A safety prompt makes agents reject >40% of safe tasks.
- **Agentic misalignment.** Press-reported, not verified: explicit "do not" rules cut blackmail from about 96% to about 37%.
- **SNARE.** The harness explains more variance than the model (56% vs 21%).

Caveats:
- External gates do not fix the underlying intent. Mind the GAP found runtime contracts had no deterrent effect on *attempts*, and the tool-affordance paper warns that guardrails mask persistent misalignment. This argues for logging attempted calls, as the `guard_tool` audit log does.
- External enforcement only works where the requirement is decidable. About 26% of requirements are not symbolically enforceable, and POLAR's reversibility pruning gave mixed results. This is the gap a calibrated verify-then-delay gate targets: the ground truth is hidden (who is Mafia?), so no static policy can decide.
- Head-to-head comparisons on identical tasks (prompt rule vs external gate vs both) are rare. GateArena and SentryBench come closest. A pre-registered, compute-matched ablation would add evidence.

## 4. What social-deduction benchmarks do NOT measure that we do

The surveyed works are Werewolf Arena, AvalonBench, MAgIC, Hoodwinked, Among Us, WOLF, Mini-Mafia, Hidden in Plain Text and MafiaScope. Mostly they measure win rate, deception production and detection, skill parameters, and, at most, suspicion calibration (WOLF Brier/ROC; MafiaScope belief calibration).

None of the works checked does any of the following:
- Treats the lynch/eliminate vote as an **irreversible action to be gated**, with a **DELAY/no-lynch abstention** option and an explicit cost of wrongful elimination.
- Evaluates **acting on calibration**: a gate driven by verified confidence, with abstention-vs-accuracy (coverage-risk) trade-offs. MafiaScope and WOLF *observe* calibration but never use it to withhold the action.
- Compares **compute-matched baselines** (CoT-x3) or **random/majority null floors** under pre-registration.
- Runs an **externally enforced gate with an audit log**, compared with in-prompt rules.
- Measures a **say-do gap** (stated suspicion or rationale vs actual vote). This exists in tool-safety work (Mind the GAP, LITMUS, AgentAbstain post-hoc abstention) but not in social deduction. Our say-do-gap novelty should be scoped to that setting.
- Makes the **transfer argument**: from game ground truth to enterprise irreversible tools.

Closest methodological neighbour: Conformal Social Choice (EMNLP 2026) does act-or-escalate on group consensus, but on QA without deception.

Possibly missed: QUACK / "Abstention Inflation" (EMNLP 2026, Werewolf and Among Us). It came up only as a search snippet and has not been checked. Verify it before claiming that no game benchmark measures abstention.
