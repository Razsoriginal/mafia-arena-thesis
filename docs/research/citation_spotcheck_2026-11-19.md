## Notes

1. Nothing in the twelve claims is numerically false. The one substantive error is the gloss in 6a: "off-lineage (different model family) monitors" misdescribes the paper. The variable is which untrusted-policy family the monitor was calibrated on, not which family the monitor model belongs to. Any argument of the form "use a monitor from a different vendor than the agent and you lose detection" is not what 2607.06596 measured. 6b (monitor pretraining lineage in ensembles) uses "lineage" in a different sense, so the pair should not be presented as two sides of one finding.

2. Abstract-only confirmations: claim 3 (GateArena) rests on the NeurIPS virtual-site abstract; OpenReview PDF returned 403. Everything else in 1, 2, 4, 5, 6 was checked against the arXiv HTML full text of the cited version, not just the abstract.

3. AgentAbstain internal inconsistency: Opus 4.7 paired accuracy appears as 59.4 (Table 1, body text) and 59.1 (App. G.1, G.4, Table 24). Gemini 3.1 Pro likewise 59.5 vs 59.4. Likely two aggregation runs/conventions; the paper does not explain it in the passages read. Quote the table you use.

4. AgentAbstain v2 restraint result, harness confound: baselines are "its frozen headline run" for each model, while "Gemini 3 Flash runs on ADK via OpenRouter for the prompted tiers". I did not find a statement of which serving stack the Gemini 3 Flash headline run used, so I cannot say the comparison is same-stack. Opus 4.7 ("Claude SDK via the Anthropic API") and DeepSeek V4 Pro ("OpenClaw via OpenRouter as in its headline run") are stated more clearly. Single rollout per task throughout.

5. Maturity of sources: 2609.29522 (IEEE submission), 2611.06116 (single author, v1, two weeks old), 2607.06596 and 2608.16190 (single-author preprints; the former a workshop paper) are not archival peer-reviewed venues. 2606.02965 is accepted at AIES 2026 but calls its own evaluation "preliminary".

6. Dates: the OpenAI page shows "Report updated: Oct 20, 2026"; the Anthropic post shows "Published Oct 9, 2026" and "Corrected Oct 12, 2026".

7. EU: the Art. 113 text was read on the Commission's AI Act Service Desk (consolidated as at 27 July 2026). The Official Journal number 2026/1744 was verified only through Publications Office metadata; the EUR-Lex text page was not readable from this environment.

## Fresh searches for a five-or-more-controls comparison

Queries used (two, as requested):

1. `paper comparing agent control mechanisms same tasks: prompt rule, self-critique, deterministic policy gate, LLM monitor, human approval, sandbox rollback — unsafe action rate and over-blocking rate 2026`
2. `arXiv 2026 benchmark "guardrail" OR "oversight mechanisms" LLM agent tool calls head-to-head comparison of defenses false refusal rate harmful action rate human-in-the-loop policy engine monitor model undo`

Result: no paper found that compares five or more distinct control types on the same tasks while reporting both a harmful-action rate and an over-blocking rate. Closest partial matches (abstract pages opened on arXiv; full texts not read, so the control counts below are from abstracts only):

- arXiv:2607.20982 GuardianAgentBench: two controls (rule-based execution-time guardrail vs system-prompt defense); "recovering 19.9% of failures at a false positive rate of just 0.5%"; 580 scenarios, six models.
- arXiv:2607.16215 RAIL Guard: pre-tool-call evaluation vs none, plus remediation variants for content; "reduces unsafe agent executions by 33% (p = 0.007) with zero impact on task completion".
- arXiv:2604.12177 Policy-Invisible Violations (PhantomPolicy): prompt-level policy, content-only DLP baseline and a graph-simulation enforcer (Sentinel); roughly three controls; balanced violation and safe-control cases.
- arXiv:2603.16586 and arXiv:2605.24309 surfaced as conceptual/taxonomy papers (search-snippet level only; not opened).
- From the claim set itself: 2606.02965 has three conditions with both safety and usability rates; 2609.29522 compares many controllers (TTL gate, confidence gate, agreement gate, random gate, three commit-time guards, routers, always-escalate) with unsafe-commit and blocked-benign rates, but they are all automated runtime guards or model-side gates on infrastructure races, with no human-approval or rollback arm; 2611.06116 varies harnesses (one of which is a reviewer) rather than control types.

Two searches are not an exhaustive literature search; "none found" means none in these result sets.
